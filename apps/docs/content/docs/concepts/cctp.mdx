---
title: CCTP
description: How Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol moves native USDC between chains by burning it on one and minting it on the other.
---

Xebra does not move USDC itself. It prices transfers, builds the transactions, and takes a fee on
some routes. The movement is Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol (CCTP), version 2.

## Burn, attest, mint

Every transfer has three steps.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Burn

The sender's wallet calls Circle's `TokenMessenger` on the chain the transfer starts on. Circle burns
the USDC and emits a message naming the destination chain, the recipient and the amount.

</Step>
<Step>

### Attest

Circle waits until the source chain has finalised the burn, then signs an attestation: a statement
that this USDC was burned for this recipient on this destination. The message and its attestation
are public. Anyone can read them from Circle's attestation service (Iris).

</Step>
<Step>

### Mint

Someone submits the message and attestation to Circle's `MessageTransmitter` on the destination. It
checks Circle's signature and mints the same amount of native USDC to the recipient, less any fee
the burn allowed. On most routes Circle's own Forwarding Service does this step; see
[Forwarding](/docs/concepts/forwarding).

</Step>
</Steps>

The USDC that arrives is minted by Circle on the destination chain. It is the same native USDC as
any other USDC there, not a wrapped or bridged token. There is no liquidity pool and no one holds
the funds in between.

## Domains

CCTP names chains by a numeric **domain**, not by chain id. A few that matter here: Solana is 5,
Sui is 8, Arc is 26 and Stellar is 27. The API and SDK use chain names (`"stellar"`, `"base"`) and
translate to domains for you. `@xebra/sdk/core` exports the full table as `EVM_CHAINS` and
`STELLAR_CCTP_DOMAIN`.

## Standard and Fast

CCTP V2 offers two finality levels, which the API calls **speeds**:

- **Standard** (`finalityThreshold` 2000). Circle attests once the source chain has finalised the
  burn. Circle charges no transfer fee for this. Its attestation never expires.
- **Fast** (`finalityThreshold` 1000). Circle attests sooner, before full finality, and charges a
  small fee proportional to the amount. Only some source chains offer it. A Fast attestation can
  expire before it is used; see [Recovery](/docs/concepts/recovery).

Stellar and Sui offer Standard only. Which speeds a route has is listed by
[`GET /v1/routes`](/docs/api/routes/list-routes), and [`GET /v1/quote`](/docs/api/quotes/get-quote)
prices one speed at a time.

## How long it takes

Most of the time is the source chain reaching finality and Circle attesting. Circle's own figures
for a Standard burn range from under a minute on chains with fast finality to about 15 to 30
minutes on others (as recorded in the SDK's chain table, `EVM_CHAINS[...].standardEta`, on
2026-11-12). Delivery after attestation usually adds seconds to a few minutes.
[`waitForDelivery`](/docs/sdk/client) polls until the transfer settles.

## Who can mint

A CCTP message can name a `destinationCaller`: the only address allowed to submit the mint.

- Burns out of Stellar and out of EVM chains leave it empty. Once Circle has attested the burn,
  anyone can submit the mint, so a transfer never depends on Xebra being online.
- Burns from Sui to Stellar name Circle's `CctpForwarder` on Stellar, which Circle requires for that
  route. The forwarder's `mint_and_forward` function is itself public, so anyone can still finish
  the transfer.

Either way the recipient and amount are fixed inside the attested message. Whoever submits the mint
pays the destination's network fee; they cannot change who receives the USDC.
