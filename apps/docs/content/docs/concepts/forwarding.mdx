---
title: Forwarding
description: Who submits the mint on the destination chain, who pays for it, and what happens when they do not.
---

A CCTP transfer is only finished when someone submits the mint on the destination chain and pays
that chain's gas. Xebra holds no funded keys for this on the routes out of Stellar and out of EVM
chains. Circle's Forwarding Service does it instead.

## Out of Stellar and EVM chains: Circle's Forwarding Service

Every burn Xebra builds out of Stellar or an EVM chain carries Circle's forwarding hook: the fixed
32 bytes `cctp-forward`, right-padded. When Circle sees that hook on an attested message, its
Forwarding Service submits the mint on the destination, pays the gas there, and takes its fee in USDC
out of the minted amount.

- The burn's `maxFee` is the most Circle may take. **Circle keeps the whole `maxFee`**, not a lower
  figure it might have quoted, so `maxFee` is a price the recipient pays, not a ceiling. Xebra signs
  Circle's own `high` forward-fee quote at 1.0x, the rule that has held on mainnet (recorded
  2026-09-20 to 2026-09-24 in the project's mainnet tests).
- Xebra bounds that fee before anyone signs: it must be above zero, at most a per-chain cap, and at
  most a tenth of the amount. On Stellar the fee contract enforces the same bounds on chain; see
  [Security](/docs/concepts/security).
- The quote reports it as `fees.delivery`. See [Fees](/docs/concepts/fees).

### New Solana recipients

USDC on Solana sits in a token account owned by a wallet. If the recipient's wallet has no USDC
account yet, Circle can open it during delivery, for a higher delivery fee that covers the account's
rent. The recipient's wallet needs no SOL for this.

That is why [`GET /v1/quote`](/docs/api/quotes/get-quote) takes an optional `recipient` for Solana:
with it, the quote includes the cost of opening the account when one is needed. Without it, the
quote assumes the account exists. [`POST /v1/transfers`](/docs/api/transfers/create-transfer)
always looks the recipient up, because the transaction it builds depends on the answer.

### When Circle does not deliver

A forward can fail: the fee moved above what was signed, or the service is down. The burn is still
attested, the funds are not lost, and the recipient is fixed in the message. The transfer's status
becomes `needs_claim` or `failed`, and anyone can submit the mint by hand. See
[Recovery](/docs/concepts/recovery).

Before pricing, the API checks Circle's forward-fee quote for the route. If Circle reports the route
as down, the API refuses to price or build a transfer (`upstream_unavailable`) rather than let
someone burn USDC that nobody would deliver. If Circle can be read but looks unwell, the quote says
so in `circleStatus: "degraded"`.

## Into Stellar: Circle's CctpForwarder

Circle's Forwarding Service does not mint on Stellar. Transfers into Stellar, which today come from
Sui, use a different contract: Circle's `CctpForwarder` on Stellar
(`CBZL2IH7F6BIDAA3WBNXYKIXSATJGMSW7K5P5MJ6STX5RXN47TZJDF5T`).

- The Sui burn mints to the forwarder and names the real Stellar recipient in its hook data.
- Once Circle has attested the burn, someone calls `mint_and_forward(message, attestation)` on the
  forwarder. It mints the USDC and pays it to the recipient named in the hook.
- Xebra runs a relayer account that makes that call and pays the Stellar fee (about 0.025 XLM per
  transfer, as of 2026-11-08). If the relayer does not, anyone can, including the recipient, with
  [`POST /v1/claims`](/docs/api/claims/create-claim).

The recipient's Stellar account needs a USDC trustline before the mint can pay it.

See [Sui to Stellar](/docs/guides/sui-to-stellar) for the whole flow.

## Not reachable yet

Some chains CCTP supports cannot be reached the way the routes above are, because Circle's Forwarding
Service does not deliver to them. Sui as a destination is one (checked against Circle's API on
2026-10-22). Xebra does not offer a route where nobody would deliver by default. The live list is
always [`GET /v1/routes`](/docs/api/routes/list-routes); see [Routes](/docs/concepts/routes).
